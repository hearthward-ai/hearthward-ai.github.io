What it does

Hearthward is a personal AI assistant, designed to run around the clock on your own computer with an open model that you download and run yourself. Instead of waiting to be asked, it is meant to remind you before something slips.

You are meant to talk to it in a chat app such as Discord, like texting a person. It answers there, and anything that acts on your behalf waits there for your OK.

A day with it (planned)

  • Morning: a short brief of what is due and what is coming up.
  • Afternoon: a reminder before a deadline slips.
  • Evening: a draft email for you to read and approve before it is sent.

Your data stays on your computer, and the model runs locally. Features like sending an email or using a chat app necessarily send that message through the service you choose, and that service can see it.

It is planned to be free to use. You need a capable PC with a modern graphics card (exact requirements will come with the release).

It is built on Hermes Agent (Nous Research), an open-source tool for running AI assistants.

It is a personal engineering project by Tony (GitHub: TonyC115), a university student. It is not a product or a company, and nobody can sign up for it yet.

Why I am building it

I wanted a 24/7 assistant for my own student life: tracking schedules and deadlines, planning my workload across the week and drafting emails. Many AI helpers that can do this cost a monthly fee and run on someone else's servers. I wanted something free and private that I, and people close to me, could trust with daily tasks.

The name: 煦坞 (xù wù) means, roughly, a warm, sheltered harbour.

For you, or an add-on for your family

Hearthward is mainly a personal tool. If you want, it could also be set up to help family members at home, each with their own limits on what it may do and whom it may contact.

A guard for outside text

An assistant that reads web pages, emails and messages can be tricked by instructions hidden in them (this is called prompt injection). Hearthward includes a guard that tries to catch and contain harmful instructions hidden in that text. It lowers the risk; it cannot remove it. It is a supporting feature, built to make the assistant safe enough to leave running. This is the planned design, and the guard is still in development. It has three parts:

Threshold

Outside text is meant to be scanned for hidden text and injection tricks, then fenced off so the model is told to treat it as data, not as orders (best effort).

Knock

By design, any action on your behalf asks first. The model only writes text; sending and changing things are meant to happen in ordinary program code (not the AI) that waits for your yes.

Gatehouse

Replies are meant to be checked on the way out, and only approved recipients can be messaged. If the guard can't confirm it is active, the assistant is designed to stay offline.

How it works

This is the planned design. The guard is in development, so it is not a finished product.

Knock applies only to actions, such as sending an email. Ordinary replies do not need your approval.

Status

Hearthward is in early development and is not released. Parts of it are being built and tested privately. Everything described on this page is the plan. There is no download yet. For how data is handled, see the privacy policy.

Planned

  • Chatting with it in a chat app (Discord first)
  • Reminders, deadlines and a daily brief
  • Sending email, only to an approved recipient list and only after you say yes
  • Reading your email inbox through the guard (this needs Google's permission to read email, so it is not done yet)
  • A workload planner that spreads tasks across the week
  • A public code release with the guard and its tests, once a complete, tested version is ready
